Projecti review:[Buddhist Studies Place Authority Databases](https://authority.dila.edu.tw/place/) 

This online repository of Buddhist texts includes rich geographical data designed to support research in Buddhist studies, Chinese historical geography, and digital humanities. Specifically, it provides:

Exact latitude and longitude of temples constructed between the 2nd and 14th centuries CE.

Geographical coordinates of cities in China spanning from the 5th century BCE to 1949.

Mapped locations of mountains, rivers, lakes, and oceans, primarily within mainland China.

Because the repository is freely accessible to the public, anyone interested in Buddhist or Chinese historical geography can search for relevant content. In addition to precise coordinates and downloadable KML files, the site includes digitized historical publications specific to each location. For example, one can not only retrieve the geographical coordinates of a particular temple, but also access detailed textual records on its historical background.

The repository draws on authoritative documentary sources, including the AuthorityProject, and is intended to assist scholars in acquiring reliable spatial data for further analysis in Buddhist geovisualization and digital humanities. It also aims to provide general users and students with foundational knowledge about historical geography in China, including Buddhist geographical development.

The site is user-friendly, offering two search forms on the left-hand side of the page: a place name search (with both general and advanced options) and a secondary search box intended for developers.

This digital archive can be a valuable teaching tool in university-level courses on Buddhism, Chinese geography, and history. Not only does it provide doctoral students and researchers with reliable data, but it also gives undergraduate students and newcomers a visual, hands-on introduction to Chinese geography through extensive map access. Users can load this data into QGIS and visualize historical locations using the latitude and longitude provided.

Limitations of the project remain and require further refinement. In particular, mapping ancient cities presents significant challenges: their locations often differ from those of modern settlements. For example, cities such as Xiapi and Sutai, dating back to the 5th century BCE, are sometimes misrepresented on the map—southern cities may be mistakenly labeled as northern, due to inconsistencies or uncertainties in historical cartography.
